what about developer that don't use Google store 

For developers not using the Google Play Store, the key is understanding that Android is an open platform. Google officially states you can distribute your apps "in any way you want, using any distribution approach or combination of approaches that meets your needs".

The main methods are divided into two categories: using other public app stores or distributing the app files directly yourself.

🛒 Public App Stores (Alternative Marketplaces)

These stores act as intermediaries, hosting your app and providing it to their user base.

· Major OEM Stores: Pre-installed on devices from specific manufacturers.
  · Samsung Galaxy Store: For Samsung device users.
  · Huawei AppGallery: Essential for reaching the Chinese market and users without Google services.
  · Xiaomi GetApps: Popular in China, India, and Indonesia.
· Independent & Niche Stores: Cater to specific audiences or philosophies.
  · Amazon Appstore: Comes pre-installed on Amazon Fire devices and is a major global alternative.
  · Aptoide: A decentralized, community-driven marketplace.
  · F-Droid: An app store dedicated exclusively to free and open-source software.

📤 Direct Distribution Methods

This involves delivering the app file (APK) to users yourself, giving you full control but requiring you to handle all aspects of distribution, updates, and security.

· Distribution via a Website: Host the APK file on your own website or server. Users download and install it directly.
· Distribution via Email: Send the APK as an email attachment to specific users.
· Self-Hosted for Testing/Internal Use: Use cloud storage (like AWS S3) or an internal server to share the app with testers or within an organization.
· Third-Party Testing Platforms: Services like Firebase App Distribution or Microsoft App Center are designed for managing beta tests and providing analytics.

⚠️ Key Considerations for Direct & Alternative Distribution

Choosing an alternative path comes with important technical and strategic trade-offs.

· User Installation Hurdle: Android blocks installations from "unknown sources" by default. Users must manually enable this setting in their device security options before installing your app. This can deter less technical users.
· Update Management: Unlike Google Play, there is no built-in auto-update system. You must create your own update mechanism (e.g., an in-app version checker that fetches a new APK from your server).
· Security & Trust: Your app won't have the implicit trust of coming from Google Play. You are responsible for securing your APK files, using code signing to prove authenticity, and educating users.
· Discovery & Marketing: You lose the organic discovery and massive audience of the Play Store. Marketing and driving users to your download point becomes entirely your responsibility.

📱 A Note on iOS (Apple) App Distribution

The situation is very different for iOS. Apple's ecosystem is historically closed, with the official App Store being the sole distribution method for most users globally.

· Recent Change in the EU: Due to the European Union's Digital Markets Act (DMA), Apple is required to allow alternative app stores for users in the EU (iOS 17.4 and later). Stores like AltStore PAL and Epic Games Store have launched there, but this is currently a regional exception.
· Global Reality: For developers targeting a global iOS audience, the Apple App Store remains the only practical public distribution channel. Enterprise programs exist for distributing private, internal apps within companies.

💡 How to Choose a Strategy

To decide which path is right for you, consider these questions about your app and goals:

· Is your app for a specific device brand (like Samsung or Huawei)? → Their official store is a logical choice.
· Are you targeting users in regions like China where Google Play is unavailable? → Local stores like Huawei AppGallery or Xiaomi GetApps are essential.
· Is your app for a limited, technical audience (like an open-source tool)? → Direct download or F-Droid might work well.
· Do you need full control over pricing, updates, and user data without any store policies? → Direct distribution from your website is an option, but be prepared for the marketing and technical challenges.
· Are you distributing an app internally to employees or to a closed beta group? → Self-hosting or a testing platform like Firebase App Distribution is ideal.
