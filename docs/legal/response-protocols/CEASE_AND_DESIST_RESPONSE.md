## II. JUS COGENS EXCEPTION (International Law Supremacy)


### What is Jus Cogens?


**Definition**: Peremptory norms of international law from which NO derogation is permitted.


**Authority**: Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (1969), Article 53


**Examples**:
- Prohibition on slavery
- Prohibition on genocide
- Prohibition on torture
- Prohibition on crimes against humanity


**Effect**: These norms are BINDING on ALL states, regardless of domestic law.


---


### The Legal Principle:


**Jus cogens > Sovereign immunity**


**Reasoning**: If a state commits jus cogens violation, it CANNOT claim immunity.


---


## III. INTERNATIONAL PRECEDENT


### A. Pinochet Case (UK House of Lords, 1999)


**Facts**: Chile's former dictator Augusto Pinochet arrested in UK for torture (committed in Chile).


**Chile's Argument**: Sovereign immunity (Pinochet was head of state).


**Holding**: **NO IMMUNITY for torture** (jus cogens violation).


**Lord Browne-Wilkinson**:
> "International law has made plain that certain types of conduct... are not acceptable conduct on the part of anyone. This applies as much to heads of state as to everyone else."


**Application to Aequitas**: If torture pierces immunity, slavery (worse crime) also pierces immunity.


---


### B. Ferrini v. Germany (Italian Supreme Court, 2004)


**Facts**: Italian citizen sued Germany for WWII forced labor.


**Germany's Argument**: State immunity under international law.


**Holding**: **NO IMMUNITY for jus cogens violations** (forced labor = slavery).


**Reasoning**:
> "When a state violates jus cogens norms, it cannot invoke immunity. The protection of fundamental human rights takes precedence."


**Application**: U.S./UK cannot claim immunity for slavery (jus cogens violation).


---


### C. Jurisdictional Immunities (ICJ, 2012)


**Case**: Germany v. Italy (International Court of Justice)


**Holding**: Jus cogens violations DO NOT automatically waive immunity BUT states should provide alternative remedy.


**Key Point**: If no alternative remedy exists, immunity CANNOT bar claims.


**Application to Aequitas**:
- U.S. has provided NO remedy for slavery
- Therefore: Immunity defense FAILS
