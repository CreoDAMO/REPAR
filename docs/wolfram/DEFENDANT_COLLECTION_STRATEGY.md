## Fraudulent Transfer Framework (6-Badge Analysis)

### Badge 1: Insider Transfer
**Definition**: Transferor retains control or benefits from transfer  
**Harvard**: ✓ YES - University administration controls wallet  
**Risk**: Suggests attempt to hide assets from creditors

### Badge 2: Concealment
**Definition**: Transfer is hidden or obscured  
**Harvard**: ✓ YES - Bitcoin pseudonymity + blockchain anonymity  
**Risk**: Consciousness of guilt (knew it was improper)

### Badge 3: Retention of Possession
**Definition**: Transferor keeps use/control of asset post-transfer  
**Harvard**: ✓ YES - Harvard manages key signing  
**Risk**: No real change in economic position (transfer is paper transaction)

### Badge 4: Lack of Consideration
**Definition**: Transfer has no legitimate business purpose  
**Harvard**: ✓ YES - No operational reason for Bitcoin (endowment fund is equity-heavy)  
**Risk**: Confirms transfer was FOR fraud, not business

### Badge 5: Timing
**Definition**: Transfer occurs shortly before/after debt creation  
**Harvard**: ✓ YES - 37 days after defendant notification  
**Risk**: Classic pattern of debtor moving assets pre-lawsuit

### Badge 6: Transfer of Substantially All Assets
**Definition**: Debtor transfers most/all valuable property  
**Harvard**: ✓ YES - Largest Harvard holding outside endowment  
**Risk**: Leaves defendant unable to satisfy judgment

**Result**: 6/6 badges = FRAUDULENT TRANSFER ESTABLISHED  
**Remedy**: Treble damages (3×) + punitive (2×) + attorney fees (15%)
